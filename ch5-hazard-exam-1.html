1
10 points

A gadget is certain to fail within 10 years, and its lifetime \(X\) (in years) is uniformly distributed on \((0,10)\).

  1. Find the survival function \(\bar F(t)\) for \(0\le t\le10\).
  2. Find the hazard rate \(\lambda(t)\), and evaluate it at \(t=5\) and \(t=9\).
  3. Explain in one sentence why \(\lambda(t)\to\infty\) as \(t\to10\).
  4. Find the probability that a 6-year-old gadget fails within the next year in two ways: directly from the uniform distribution, and from \(e^{-\int\lambda}\).
Solution

(a) \(\bar F(t)=1-\tfrac{t}{10}=\tfrac{10-t}{10}\).

(b) \(\lambda(t)=\dfrac{f(t)}{\bar F(t)}=\dfrac{1/10}{(10-t)/10}=\dfrac1{10-t}\). \(\lambda(5)=0.2\), \(\lambda(9)=1\) per year.

(c) A gadget still working near age 10 must fail in the little time left, so the failure intensity for survivors grows without bound.

(d) Directly: \(P\{X\le7\mid X>6\}=\dfrac{1/10}{4/10}=\tfrac14\). With the hazard: \(\int_6^7\tfrac{ds}{10-s}=\ln\tfrac43\), so \(1-e^{-\ln(4/3)}=1-\tfrac34=\tfrac14\).

2
10 points

The time \(T\) (in years) until a newly opened restaurant closes has CDF \(F(t)=\dfrac{t^2}{1+t^2}\) for \(t\ge0\).

  1. Find the survival function and the density of \(T\).
  2. Find the hazard rate \(\lambda(t)\).
  3. At what age is the hazard rate highest, and what is its value there? Describe how the hazard behaves for young and for old restaurants.
  4. For a restaurant that has been open exactly 1 year, approximate the probability that it closes in the next 0.1 year using \(\lambda(1)\), then compute it exactly.
Solution

(a) \(\bar F(t)=\dfrac1{1+t^2}\), \(f(t)=F'(t)=\dfrac{2t}{(1+t^2)^2}\).

(b) \(\lambda(t)=\dfrac{f(t)}{\bar F(t)}=\dfrac{2t}{1+t^2}\).

(c) \(\lambda'(t)=\dfrac{2(1-t^2)}{(1+t^2)^2}\), zero at \(t=1\), where \(\lambda(1)=1\) per year. The hazard starts at 0, rises over the first year, then falls toward 0: restaurants that make it past the risky early period become steadily safer.

(d) Approximation: \(\lambda(1)\cdot0.1=0.1\). Exact: \(1-\dfrac{\bar F(1.1)}{\bar F(1)}=1-\dfrac{2}{2.21}\approx0.0950\).

3
10 points

Drivers who text behind the wheel have, at every point in time, three times the hazard rate of having their first accident as drivers who do not. A non-texting driver has probability 0.8 of going 5 years without an accident.

  1. Find the probability that a texting driver goes 5 years without an accident.
  2. If the texting hazard were \(k\) times the non-texting hazard instead, what \(k\) would make a texting driver's 5-year accident-free probability exactly one half?
  3. An insurer's pool is 20% texters. Find the probability that a randomly chosen driver from the pool goes 5 years without an accident.
  4. A driver from the pool has been accident-free for 5 years. Find the probability that the driver texts.
Solution

Let \(\Lambda=\int_0^5\lambda_n(t)\,dt\). Then \(e^{-\Lambda}=0.8\), and the texter's survival is \(e^{-3\Lambda}=(e^{-\Lambda})^3\).

(a) \(0.8^3=0.512\). Tripling the hazard cubes the survival probability; it does not divide it by 3.

(b) \(0.8^k=0.5\Rightarrow k=\dfrac{\ln0.5}{\ln0.8}\approx3.1063\).

(c) \(0.2(0.512)+0.8(0.8)=0.7424\).

(d) \(\dfrac{0.2(0.512)}{0.7424}=\dfrac{0.1024}{0.7424}\approx0.1379\), down from the prior 0.2.

4
10 points

A type of electronic unit has a “bathtub” hazard rate (in failures per year): $$\lambda(t)=\begin{cases}0.3-0.1t, & 0\le t<2\\ 0.1, & 2\le t<8\\ 0.1+0.05(t-8), & t\ge8.\end{cases}$$

  1. Find the probability that a new unit survives its first 2 years.
  2. Given that a unit survives its first 2 years, find the probability that it is still working at age 8.
  3. Find the probability that a unit working at age 8 fails before age 10.
  4. Find the density of the lifetime at \(t=1\).
Solution

Use \(P\{\text{alive at }b\mid\text{alive at }a\}=e^{-\int_a^b\lambda(t)dt}\).

(a) \(\int_0^2(0.3-0.1t)dt=0.6-0.2=0.4\), so \(e^{-0.4}\approx0.6703\).

(b) \(\int_2^8 0.1\,dt=0.6\), so \(e^{-0.6}\approx0.5488\).

(c) \(\int_8^{10}\big(0.1+0.05(t-8)\big)dt=0.2+0.1=0.3\), so \(1-e^{-0.3}\approx0.2592\).

(d) \(\int_0^1(0.3-0.1t)dt=0.25\), so \(f(1)=\lambda(1)\bar F(1)=0.2\,e^{-0.25}\approx0.1558\).

5
10 points

A repair job has two stages, and the total time \(T\) (in hours) to complete it has density \(f(t)=t\,e^{-t}\) for \(t>0\).

  1. Find the survival function \(\bar F(t)\).
  2. Find the hazard rate \(\lambda(t)\).
  3. Given that a job is not finished after 2 hours, find the probability that it is finished within the next hour.
  4. What does \(\lambda(t)\) approach as \(t\to\infty\)? Compare with an exponential repair time with mean 1 hour.
Solution

(a) Integrating by parts, \(\bar F(t)=\int_t^\infty se^{-s}ds=\big[-se^{-s}-e^{-s}\big]_t^\infty=(1+t)e^{-t}\).

(b) \(\lambda(t)=\dfrac{te^{-t}}{(1+t)e^{-t}}=\dfrac{t}{1+t}\). It starts at 0 and increases.

(c) \(1-\dfrac{\bar F(3)}{\bar F(2)}=1-\dfrac{4e^{-3}}{3e^{-2}}=1-\tfrac43e^{-1}\approx0.5095\).

(d) \(\lambda(t)\to1\). A long-running job behaves like an exponential job with rate 1 (mean 1 hour); early on the hazard is lower because both stages must finish first.

6
10 points

Machine A has constant hazard rate 0.1 per year. Machine B has hazard rate \(0.02t\) per year at age \(t\).

  1. Find each machine's probability of surviving 5 years.
  2. At what age are the two hazard rates equal? Which machine has the higher hazard after that age?
  3. At what age are the two survival probabilities equal?
  4. For a 10-year-old machine of each type, find the probability that it survives 2 more years. Explain why the answers differ even though both have the same chance of reaching age 10.
Solution

Survival: \(\bar F_A(t)=e^{-0.1t}\), \(\bar F_B(t)=e^{-0.01t^2}\).

(a) A: \(e^{-0.5}\approx0.6065\). B: \(e^{-0.25}\approx0.7788\).

(b) \(0.02t=0.1\Rightarrow t=5\). After age 5, B has the higher hazard.

(c) \(0.1t=0.01t^2\Rightarrow t=10\). The hazards cross at 5 but the survival curves only cross at 10, because survival depends on the accumulated hazard \(\int_0^t\lambda\), not the current value.

(d) A: \(e^{-0.1(2)}=e^{-0.2}\approx0.8187\) (memoryless). B: \(e^{-0.01(144-100)}=e^{-0.44}\approx0.6440\). B wears out: at age 10 its hazard is 0.2 and climbing, twice A's.

7
10 points

For people aged 40 and over, an actuary models the hazard rate of death at age \(t\) (in years) as \(\lambda(t)=0.001\,e^{0.09(t-40)}\).

  1. Find the probability that a 40-year-old reaches age 70.
  2. Approximate the probability that a 60-year-old dies within the next month.
  3. How many years does it take for the hazard rate to double?
  4. At what age does a 40-year-old have a 50% chance of still being alive?
Solution

\(\int_{40}^{x}\lambda(t)dt=\dfrac{0.001}{0.09}\left(e^{0.09(x-40)}-1\right)=\dfrac{e^{0.09(x-40)}-1}{90}\).

(a) \(\dfrac{e^{2.7}-1}{90}\approx0.1542\), so \(e^{-0.1542}\approx0.8571\).

(b) \(\lambda(60)=0.001e^{1.8}\approx0.006050\) per year, so \(\approx\lambda(60)\cdot\tfrac1{12}\approx0.000504\). (Exact: 0.000506.)

(c) \(e^{0.09d}=2\Rightarrow d=\tfrac{\ln2}{0.09}\approx7.70\) years.

(d) \(\dfrac{e^{0.09u}-1}{90}=\ln2\Rightarrow u=\dfrac{\ln(1+90\ln2)}{0.09}\approx46.1\), so about age 86.1.

8
10 points

A delivery drone fails if either its motor or its flight controller fails, and the two fail independently. The motor's hazard rate is \(t^2/1000\) and the controller's is a constant 0.02, both per hour of flight time \(t\).

  1. Show that the drone's hazard rate is the sum of the two component hazard rates.
  2. Find the probability that the drone flies 10 hours without failure.
  3. Given that the drone has flown 10 hours without failure, find the probability that it reaches 15 hours.
  4. At what flight time do the motor and the controller contribute equally to the drone's hazard? Which dominates for a new drone?
Solution

(a) The drone's lifetime is \(\min(M,C)\), so \(\bar F(t)=P\{M>t\}P\{C>t\}=e^{-\int_0^t\lambda_M}e^{-\int_0^t\lambda_C}=e^{-\int_0^t(\lambda_M+\lambda_C)}\). Hence \(\lambda(t)=\tfrac{t^2}{1000}+0.02\), and \(\bar F(t)=e^{-0.02t-t^3/3000}\).

(b) \(e^{-0.2-1/3}=e^{-0.5333}\approx0.5866\).

(c) \(\exp\left(-0.02(5)-\tfrac{15^3-10^3}{3000}\right)=e^{-0.1-0.7917}\approx0.4100\).

(d) \(\tfrac{t^2}{1000}=0.02\Rightarrow t=\sqrt{20}\approx4.47\) hours. Before that the controller dominates; for a new drone the motor's hazard is near 0.

9
10 points

Subscribers to a streaming service are of two kinds: 40% are “samplers”, who cancel at a constant hazard rate of 0.5 per month, and 60% are “loyal”, who cancel at a constant hazard rate of 0.1 per month. A new subscriber's type is not known.

  1. Find the probability that a new subscriber is still subscribed after 6 months.
  2. Find the hazard rate of cancellation for a new subscriber at \(t=0\) and at \(t=6\) months.
  3. Given that a subscriber is still subscribed after 6 months, find the probability that they are a sampler.
  4. Explain why the overall hazard decreases with time even though each type has a constant hazard.
Solution

(a) \(\bar F(t)=0.4e^{-0.5t}+0.6e^{-0.1t}\), so \(\bar F(6)=0.4e^{-3}+0.6e^{-0.6}\approx0.3492\).

(b) \(f(t)=0.2e^{-0.5t}+0.06e^{-0.1t}\). \(\lambda(0)=\tfrac{0.26}{1}=0.26\). \(\lambda(6)=\dfrac{0.2e^{-3}+0.06e^{-0.6}}{0.3492}\approx0.1228\).

(c) \(\dfrac{0.4e^{-3}}{0.3492}\approx0.0570\).

(d) \(\lambda(t)=0.5\,P\{\text{sampler}\mid T>t\}+0.1\,P\{\text{loyal}\mid T>t\}\). As time passes the samplers cancel and the survivors are mostly loyal, so the mix shifts toward the lower rate. Check at \(t=6\): \(0.5(0.0570)+0.1(0.9430)\approx0.1228\).

10
10 points

A component's hazard rate has the form \(\lambda(t)=c\,t^{k}\) (years), with unknown \(c>0\) and \(k>-1\). Test data show that 90% of components survive 1 year and 40% survive 3 years.

  1. Write the survival function in terms of \(c\) and \(k\).
  2. Find \(k\) and \(c\).
  3. Is the hazard increasing or decreasing? What does that say about these components?
  4. Find the probability that a 3-year-old component survives to age 4.
Solution

(a) \(\bar F(t)=\exp\left(-\dfrac{c}{k+1}t^{k+1}\right)\).

(b) Let \(a=k+1\) and \(b=c/a\). Then \(b=-\ln0.9\approx0.10536\) and \(b\,3^{a}=-\ln0.4\approx0.91629\). Dividing, \(3^{a}=8.6967\), so \(a=\dfrac{\ln8.6967}{\ln3}\approx1.9688\), giving \(k\approx0.9688\) and \(c=ab\approx0.2074\).

(c) \(k>0\), so the hazard increases (almost linearly): the components wear out with age.

(d) \(\exp\left(-0.10536\left(4^{1.9688}-3^{1.9688}\right)\right)\approx0.4975\).
