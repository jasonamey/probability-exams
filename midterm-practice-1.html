Of the visitors to a website, 10% are automated bots and 90% are humans. Each visitor is shown a sequence of ads. A bot clicks any given ad with probability 0.6 and a human with probability 0.05; given the type of visitor, clicks on different ads are independent.

  1. Find the probability that a randomly chosen visitor clicks the first ad.
  2. Find the probability that a visitor clicks both the first and the second ad. Are the events "clicks the first ad" and "clicks the second ad" independent? Explain why or why not.
  3. A visitor is shown 4 ads and clicks exactly 2 of them. Find the probability that the visitor is a bot.
  4. The visitor in (c) is then shown a fifth ad. Find the probability that they click it.
Solution

Let \(B\) be the event that the visitor is a bot and \(C_i\) the event that they click ad \(i\). Then \(P(B)=0.1\), \(P(C_i\mid B)=0.6\), \(P(C_i\mid B^c)=0.05\), and the \(C_i\) are conditionally independent given \(B\) and given \(B^c\).

(a) \(P(C_1)=0.6(0.1)+0.05(0.9)=0.06+0.045=0.105\).

(b) \(P(C_1C_2)=0.6^2(0.1)+0.05^2(0.9)=0.036+0.00225=0.03825\). Since \(P(C_1)P(C_2)=0.105^2=0.011025\ne0.03825\), they are not independent. A click on the first ad makes it more likely the visitor is a bot, which raises the chance of the second click: \(P(C_2\mid C_1)=0.03825/0.105=0.3643\) versus \(P(C_2)=0.105\). Conditional independence does not give independence.

(c) Given the type, the number of clicks out of 4 is binomial. \(P(2\text{ clicks}\mid B)=\binom42(0.6)^2(0.4)^2=0.3456\) and \(P(2\text{ clicks}\mid B^c)=\binom42(0.05)^2(0.95)^2=0.0135375\). By Bayes, $$P(B\mid 2\text{ clicks})=\frac{0.3456(0.1)}{0.3456(0.1)+0.0135375(0.9)}=\frac{0.03456}{0.0467438}=0.7394.$$

(d) Condition on the type using the updated probabilities from (c): \(0.7394(0.6)+0.2606(0.05)=0.4566\).

16 points

A random variable \(X\) has distribution function $$F(x)=\begin{cases}0, & x<0\\ 0.2+0.3x, & 0\le x<1\\ 0.6, & 1\le x<3\\ 1-0.4e^{-(x-3)}, & x\ge 3.\end{cases}$$

  1. Is \(X\) discrete, continuous, or neither? Justify.
  2. Find \(P(X=0)\), \(P(X=1)\) and \(P(X=3)\).
  3. Find \(P(0<X\le1)\) and \(P(\tfrac12\le X<4)\).
  4. Find \(E[X]\) and \(P(X>4\mid X>1)\).
Solution

(a) Neither. \(F\) jumps at \(x=0\) and \(x=1\), so \(X\) is not continuous, and \(F\) increases continuously on \((0,1)\) and on \((3,\infty)\), so it is not a step function and \(X\) is not discrete.

(b) Use \(P(X=a)=F(a)-F(a^-)\). \(P(X=0)=0.2-0=0.2\); \(P(X=1)=0.6-(0.2+0.3)=0.1\); \(P(X=3)=(1-0.4)-0.6=0\).

(c) \(P(0<X\le1)=F(1)-F(0)=0.6-0.2=0.4\). \(P(\tfrac12\le X<4)=F(4^-)-F(\tfrac12^-)=(1-0.4e^{-1})-0.35=0.65-0.1472=0.5028\).

(d) The distribution has point masses 0.2 at 0 and 0.1 at 1, density \(0.3\) on \((0,1)\), and density \(0.4e^{-(x-3)}\) on \((3,\infty)\). Then $$E[X]=0(0.2)+1(0.1)+\int_0^1 0.3x\,dx+\int_3^\infty 0.4x e^{-(x-3)}\,dx=0.1+0.15+0.4(4)=1.85,$$ using \(\int_3^\infty xe^{-(x-3)}dx=\int_0^\infty(u+3)e^{-u}du=1+3=4\). Finally \(P(X>4\mid X>1)=\dfrac{1-F(4)}{1-F(1)}=\dfrac{0.4e^{-1}}{0.4}=e^{-1}=0.3679\).

18 points

Eight cards are dealt from a well-shuffled standard 52-card deck (13 cards in each of 4 suits). For \(i=1,2,3,4\), let \(A_i\) be the event that the hand contains no card of suit \(i\).

  1. Find \(P(A_i)\).
  2. Find \(P(A_iA_j)\) for \(i\ne j\) and \(P(A_iA_jA_k)\) for distinct \(i,j,k\). What is \(P(A_1A_2A_3A_4)\)?
  3. Find the probability that the hand is missing at least one suit.
  4. Find the probability that the hand is missing exactly one suit. Given that the hand is missing at least one suit, what is the probability that it is missing exactly one?
Solution

All \(\binom{52}{8}=752{,}538{,}150\) hands are equally likely.

(a) \(P(A_i)=\binom{39}{8}\big/\binom{52}{8}=61{,}523{,}748/752{,}538{,}150=0.0818\).

(b) \(P(A_iA_j)=\binom{26}{8}\big/\binom{52}{8}=1{,}562{,}275/752{,}538{,}150=0.0021\). \(P(A_iA_jA_k)=\binom{13}{8}\big/\binom{52}{8}=1287/752{,}538{,}150\approx1.7\times10^{-6}\). \(P(A_1A_2A_3A_4)=0\): a hand of 8 cards must contain some suit.

(c) By inclusion–exclusion, $$P\Big(\bigcup A_i\Big)=\frac{4\binom{39}{8}-6\binom{26}{8}+4\binom{13}{8}}{\binom{52}{8}}=\frac{236{,}726{,}490}{752{,}538{,}150}=0.3146.$$

(d) Choose the missing suit (4 ways), then count 8-card hands from the other 39 cards that contain all three remaining suits, again by inclusion–exclusion: \(\binom{39}{8}-3\binom{26}{8}+3\binom{13}{8}\). So $$P(\text{exactly one missing})=\frac{4\left[\binom{39}{8}-3\binom{26}{8}+3\binom{13}{8}\right]}{\binom{52}{8}}=\frac{227{,}363{,}136}{752{,}538{,}150}=0.3021.$$ The conditional probability is \(0.3021/0.3146=0.9604\): almost every hand that misses a suit misses only one.

16 points

For some constant \(c\), a random variable \(X\) has density \(f(x)=cx^2(1-x)\) for \(0<x<1\) and \(f(x)=0\) otherwise.

  1. Find \(c\).
  2. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  3. Find the probability that \(X\) lies within one standard deviation of its mean.
  4. Find \(E[1/X]\). Is it equal to \(1/E[X]\)?
Solution

(a) \(\int_0^1(x^2-x^3)dx=\tfrac13-\tfrac14=\tfrac1{12}\), so \(c=12\).

(b) \(E[X]=12\int_0^1(x^3-x^4)dx=12\left(\tfrac14-\tfrac15\right)=\tfrac35\). \(E[X^2]=12\left(\tfrac15-\tfrac16\right)=\tfrac25\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac25-\tfrac9{25}=\tfrac1{25}\), so \(\sigma=\tfrac15\).

(c) For \(0\le x\le1\), \(F(x)=12\left(\tfrac{x^3}3-\tfrac{x^4}4\right)=4x^3-3x^4\). \(P(0.4<X<0.8)=F(0.8)-F(0.4)=(2.048-1.2288)-(0.256-0.0768)=0.8192-0.1792=0.64\).

(d) \(E[1/X]=\int_0^1\tfrac1x\cdot12x^2(1-x)dx=12\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac13\right)=2\), while \(1/E[X]=\tfrac53\). They are not equal: \(E[g(X)]\ne g(E[X])\) in general.

17 points

A committee of 5 is chosen at random from a group of 8 women and 7 men. Let \(X\) be the number of women on the committee.

  1. Find \(P(X=k)\) for \(k=0,\dots,5\) (a formula is fine) and compute \(P(X\ge3)\).
  2. Find \(E[X]\) by writing \(X\) as a sum of indicator variables. Then find \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  3. Given that the committee contains at least one man, find the probability that it contains exactly four women.
  4. The committee then chooses a chair and a vice-chair at random from its 5 members. Find the probability that both are women.
Solution

(a) \(P(X=k)=\binom8k\binom7{5-k}\big/\binom{15}5\), with \(\binom{15}5=3003\). \(P(X\ge3)=\dfrac{\binom83\binom72+\binom84\binom71+\binom85}{3003}=\dfrac{1176+490+56}{3003}=\dfrac{1722}{3003}=\dfrac{82}{143}=0.5734\).

(b) Let \(I_i\) indicate that woman \(i\) is chosen, so \(X=\sum_{i=1}^8 I_i\) and \(P(I_i=1)=\binom{14}4/\binom{15}5=\tfrac13\). Then \(E[X]=8/3\). For \(i\ne j\), \(P(I_iI_j=1)=\binom{13}3/\binom{15}5=\tfrac{2}{21}\), so \(E[X^2]=E[X]+2\binom82\tfrac{2}{21}=\tfrac83+\tfrac{16}3=8\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=8-\tfrac{64}9=\tfrac89\).

(c) \(P(X=4\mid X\le4)=\dfrac{P(X=4)}{1-P(X=5)}=\dfrac{490/3003}{1-56/3003}=\dfrac{490}{2947}=\dfrac{70}{421}=0.1663\).

(d) By symmetry the chair is equally likely to be any of the 15 people, and the vice-chair any of the other 14, so the probability is \(\tfrac8{15}\cdot\tfrac7{14}=\tfrac4{15}\). Conditioning on \(X\) gives the same answer: \(\sum_k P(X=k)\tfrac{k(k-1)}{20}=\tfrac{E[X^2]-E[X]}{20}=\tfrac{8-8/3}{20}=\tfrac4{15}\).

16 points

A vaccine is given to 2000 people. Each person, independently, has a severe reaction with probability 0.001. Let \(X\) be the number of severe reactions.

  1. Find the exact probability of no severe reactions, and of at most two.
  2. Find the expected number of severe reactions and the standard deviation.
  3. Repeat (a) using an appropriate approximation, and explain why it is reasonable here.
  4. The same vaccine is given to 2000 people at each of five clinics. Find the probability that at least one clinic records four or more severe reactions.
Solution

\(X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(2000,0.001)\).

(a) \(P(X=0)=0.999^{2000}=0.1352\). \(P(X\le2)=0.999^{2000}+2000(0.001)(0.999)^{1999}+\binom{2000}2(0.001)^2(0.999)^{1998}=0.1352+0.2707+0.2708=0.6767\).

(b) \(E[X]=np=2\); \(\mathrm{SD}=\sqrt{np(1-p)}=\sqrt{1.998}=1.4135\).

(c) \(n\) is large and \(p\) is small with \(np=2\) moderate, so \(X\approx\mathrm{Po}(2)\). \(P(X=0)\approx e^{-2}=0.1353\) and \(P(X\le2)\approx e^{-2}(1+2+2)=5e^{-2}=0.6767\), matching the exact answers to 4 decimals.

(d) For one clinic, \(P(X\ge4)\approx1-e^{-2}\left(1+2+2+\tfrac43\right)=1-\tfrac{19}3e^{-2}=0.1429\). The clinics are independent, so the number of clinics with four or more is \(\mathrm{Bin}(5,0.1429)\) and \(P(\text{at least one})=1-(0.8571)^5=0.5374\).
