For some constant \(c\), a random variable \(X\) has density \(f(x)=c\sqrt x\) for \(0<x<4\) and \(f(x)=0\) otherwise.

  1. Find \(c\).
  2. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  3. Find the median of \(X\) and \(P(X>E[X])\).
  4. Let \(Y=\sqrt X\). Find the density of \(Y\), and compute \(E[Y]\) in two ways: from the density of \(Y\), and directly from the density of \(X\).
Solution

(a) \(\int_0^4\sqrt x\,dx=\tfrac23\cdot8=\tfrac{16}3\), so \(c=\tfrac3{16}\).

(b) \(E[X]=\tfrac3{16}\int_0^4x^{3/2}dx=\tfrac3{16}\cdot\tfrac25\cdot32=\tfrac{12}5\). \(E[X^2]=\tfrac3{16}\cdot\tfrac27\cdot128=\tfrac{48}7\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{48}7-\tfrac{144}{25}=\tfrac{192}{175}=1.0971\).

(c) \(F(x)=\tfrac3{16}\cdot\tfrac23x^{3/2}=\tfrac{x^{3/2}}8\) on \([0,4]\). The median solves \(m^{3/2}/8=\tfrac12\), so \(m=4^{2/3}=2.5198\). \(P(X>2.4)=1-\tfrac{2.4^{3/2}}8=1-\tfrac{3.7181}8=0.5352\). The mean is below the median because the density leans to the right.

(d) For \(0<y<2\), \(F_Y(y)=P(X\le y^2)=\tfrac{y^3}8\), so \(f_Y(y)=\tfrac{3y^2}8\) on \((0,2)\) (or by Theorem 5.7.1, \(f_X(y^2)\cdot2y=\tfrac3{16}y\cdot2y\)). Then \(E[Y]=\int_0^2\tfrac{3y^3}8dy=\tfrac38\cdot4=\tfrac32\). Directly, \(E[\sqrt X]=\int_0^4\sqrt x\cdot\tfrac3{16}\sqrt x\,dx=\tfrac3{16}\cdot8=\tfrac32\).

17 points

Of the items coming off a production line, 5% are defective. Each item is inspected twice by independent scanners. A scanner flags a defective item with probability 0.9 and wrongly flags a good item with probability 0.1. Given whether an item is defective, the two scans are independent.

  1. Find the probability that the first scan flags a randomly chosen item.
  2. Find the probability that both scans flag the item. Are the two flags independent?
  3. Find the probability that an item is defective given that both scans flag it, and given that exactly one scan flags it.
  4. Suppose the item is scanned \(k\) times and every scan flags it. What is the smallest \(k\) for which the probability that it is defective exceeds 0.95?
Solution

Let \(D\) be the event that the item is defective and \(G_i\) the event that scan \(i\) flags it.

(a) \(P(G_1)=0.9(0.05)+0.1(0.95)=0.045+0.095=0.14\).

(b) \(P(G_1G_2)=0.9^2(0.05)+0.1^2(0.95)=0.0405+0.0095=0.05\). Since \(0.14^2=0.0196\ne0.05\), the flags are not independent; they are only conditionally independent given \(D\) or \(D^c\).

(c) \(P(D\mid G_1G_2)=0.0405/0.05=0.81\). For exactly one flag, \(P(\text{one}\mid D)=2(0.9)(0.1)=0.18\) and \(P(\text{one}\mid D^c)=2(0.1)(0.9)=0.18\), so $$P(D\mid\text{exactly one})=\frac{0.18(0.05)}{0.18(0.05)+0.18(0.95)}=0.05.$$ One flag and one clear are equally likely either way, so they carry no information and the probability stays at the prior.

(d) The posterior odds of \(D\) after \(k\) flags are \(\dfrac{0.05}{0.95}\cdot\left(\dfrac{0.9}{0.1}\right)^k=\dfrac{9^k}{19}\). A probability above 0.95 means odds above 19, so we need \(9^k>361\). \(9^2=81\) is too small and \(9^3=729\) is enough, so \(k=3\). Check: \(P(D\mid 3\text{ flags})=\dfrac{729}{729+19}=0.9746\), while two flags give 0.81.

17 points

Potholes occur along a highway at an average rate of 1.5 per kilometre. They are scattered at random: the numbers of potholes in non-overlapping stretches are independent, and the chance of a pothole in a very short stretch of length \(h\) is about \(1.5h\).

  1. Find the probability that a 2-km stretch has no potholes.
  2. Given that a particular 1-km stretch has at least one pothole, find the probability that it has at least three.
  3. A road crew starts at kilometre 0 and drives forward. Let \(D\) be the distance to the first pothole. Find the distribution of \(D\) and \(P(D>1)\). Given that there is no pothole in the first kilometre, find the probability that there is none in the next half kilometre.
  4. Each pothole, independently of the others, is severe with probability 0.2. Find the probability that a 5-km stretch contains no severe pothole. (Condition on the total number of potholes.)
Solution

The number of potholes in a stretch of length \(t\) km is \(\mathrm{Po}(1.5t)\).

(a) \(\mathrm{Po}(3)\): \(e^{-3}=0.0498\).

(b) With \(N\sim\mathrm{Po}(1.5)\), \(P(N\ge3)=1-e^{-1.5}(1+1.5+1.125)=1-3.625e^{-1.5}=0.1912\) and \(P(N\ge1)=1-e^{-1.5}=0.7769\). So \(P(N\ge3\mid N\ge1)=0.1912/0.7769=0.2461\).

(c) \(P(D>d)=P(\text{no pothole in }[0,d])=e^{-1.5d}\), so \(D\) is exponential with rate 1.5 (mean \(\tfrac23\) km). \(P(D>1)=e^{-1.5}=0.2231\). By the memoryless property (or by independence of disjoint stretches), \(P(D>1.5\mid D>1)=P(D>0.5)=e^{-0.75}=0.4724\).

(d) Let \(N\sim\mathrm{Po}(7.5)\) be the number of potholes in 5 km. Given \(N=n\), the probability that none is severe is \(0.8^n\). So $$P(\text{no severe})=\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-7.5}\frac{7.5^n}{n!}0.8^n=e^{-7.5}e^{6}=e^{-1.5}=0.2231.$$ Severe potholes therefore behave like the same kind of process with rate \(0.2(1.5)=0.3\) per km.

16 points

An integer is chosen uniformly at random from \(\{1,2,\dots,1000\}\).

  1. Find the probability that it is divisible by both 2 and 3.
  2. Find the probability that it is divisible by at least one of 2, 3, 5.
  3. Find the probability that it is divisible by exactly one of 2, 3, 5.
  4. Three integers are chosen independently, each uniformly from \(\{1,\dots,1000\}\). Find the probability that at least one of them is divisible by none of 2, 3, 5.
Solution

Let \(A_d\) be the event that the integer is divisible by \(d\). The number of multiples of \(d\) up to 1000 is \(\lfloor1000/d\rfloor\): 500, 333, 200 for \(d=2,3,5\); 166, 100, 66 for \(d=6,10,15\); 33 for \(d=30\).

(a) Divisible by 2 and 3 means divisible by 6: \(166/1000=0.166\).

(b) \(P(A_2\cup A_3\cup A_5)=\dfrac{500+333+200-166-100-66+33}{1000}=\dfrac{734}{1000}=0.734\).

(c) With \(S_1=1033\), \(S_2=332\), \(S_3=33\) (counts of single, pairwise and triple intersections), the number divisible by exactly one is \(S_1-2S_2+3S_3=1033-664+99=468\), so the probability is 0.468. (Each integer in exactly two of the sets is counted twice in \(S_1\) and once in \(S_2\); each integer in all three is counted 3, 3 and 1 times; the coefficients remove both.)

(d) Each integer is divisible by none of them with probability \(1-0.734=0.266\), independently. \(P(\text{at least one})=1-0.734^3=1-0.3954=0.6046\).

About 7% of blood donors have type O-negative blood. A blood drive collects from 500 unrelated donors. Let \(X\) be the number of O-negative donors.

  1. Find the mean and standard deviation of \(X\), and explain why a normal approximation is reasonable.
  2. Approximate the probability that at least 40 donors are O-negative.
  3. Approximate the probability that between 30 and 40 donors (inclusive) are O-negative.
  4. The hospital needs at least 40 O-negative donations. How many donors must the drive collect so that the probability of meeting that need is at least 0.95?
Solution

(a) \(X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(500,0.07)\): \(E[X]=35\), \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=500(0.07)(0.93)=32.55\), \(\mathrm{SD}=5.7053\). Since \(np(1-p)=32.55\ge10\), the DeMoivre–Laplace approximation applies.

(b) With continuity correction, \(P(X\ge40)=P(X\ge39.5)\approx1-\Phi\!\left(\dfrac{39.5-35}{5.7053}\right)=1-\Phi(0.79)=1-0.7852=0.2148\). (Exact binomial: 0.2123.)

(c) \(P(30\le X\le40)=P(29.5\le X\le40.5)\approx\Phi(0.96)-\Phi(-0.96)=2(0.8315)-1=0.6630\), since \(\pm5.5/5.7053=\pm0.96\).

(d) With \(n\) donors we need \(P(X\ge39.5)\ge0.95\), that is \(\dfrac{39.5-0.07n}{\sqrt{0.0651n}}\le-1.645\). Put \(s=\sqrt n\) and use \(\sqrt{0.0651}=0.25515\): \(0.07s^2-0.41972s-39.5\ge0\). The positive root is $$s=\frac{0.41972+\sqrt{0.41972^2+4(0.07)(39.5)}}{2(0.07)}=\frac{0.41972+3.3521}{0.14}=26.94,$$ so \(n\ge725.9\): the drive needs 726 donors. (The exact binomial probability with 726 donors is 0.954.)

A random variable \(X\) has distribution function $$F(x)=\begin{cases}0, & x<-1\\ \tfrac14+\tfrac{x+1}8, & -1\le x<1\\ \tfrac34, & 1\le x<2\\ 1, & x\ge2.\end{cases}$$

  1. Is \(X\) discrete, continuous, or neither? Justify.
  2. Find every value \(a\) with \(P(X=a)>0\), together with these probabilities. What is \(P(X=0)\)?
  3. Find \(P(-1<X<1.5)\), \(P(|X|<1)\) and \(P(X\le0\mid X<2)\).
  4. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
Solution

(a) Neither: \(F\) has jumps (so \(X\) is not continuous) but rises continuously on \((-1,1)\) (so \(F\) is not a step function).

(b) Jumps: at \(-1\), \(F(-1)-F(-1^-)=\tfrac14\); at 1, \(\tfrac34-\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac28\right)=\tfrac14\); at 2, \(1-\tfrac34=\tfrac14\). \(F\) is continuous at 0, so \(P(X=0)=0\). The remaining mass \(\tfrac14\) is spread with density \(\tfrac18\) on \((-1,1)\).

(c) \(P(-1<X<1.5)=F(1.5^-)-F(-1)=\tfrac34-\tfrac14=\tfrac12\) (it includes the mass at 1). \(P(|X|<1)=F(1^-)-F(-1)=\tfrac12-\tfrac14=\tfrac14\) (it excludes both point masses). \(P(X\le0\mid X<2)=\dfrac{F(0)}{F(2^-)}=\dfrac{3/8}{3/4}=\tfrac12\).

(d) \(E[X]=-1\cdot\tfrac14+1\cdot\tfrac14+2\cdot\tfrac14+\int_{-1}^1\tfrac x8dx=\tfrac12\). \(E[X^2]=\tfrac14+\tfrac14+1+\int_{-1}^1\tfrac{x^2}8dx=\tfrac32+\tfrac1{12}=\tfrac{19}{12}\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{19}{12}-\tfrac14=\tfrac43\).
