A device contains three components. Components 1 and 2 are wired in series, and that pair is wired in parallel with component 3, so the device works if both 1 and 2 work or if 3 works. The components work independently with probabilities 0.9, 0.8, and 0.7.

  1. Find the probability that the device works.
  2. Given that the device works, find the probability that component 3 has failed.
  3. Are the events “the device works” and “component 3 works” independent? Justify with numbers.
Solution

(a) \(P(\text{1 and 2 work})=0.72\). \(P(\text{works})=1-(1-0.72)(1-0.7)=1-0.084=0.916\).

(b) The device works with 3 failed only if 1 and 2 both work: \(\dfrac{0.72(0.3)}{0.916}\approx0.2358\).

(c) No. \(P(\text{works}\mid\text{3 works})=1\ne0.916\).

6 points

Consider the ten letters of the word STATISTICS.

  1. How many different arrangements of the ten letters are there?
  2. How many arrangements have the two I's next to each other?
  3. How many arrangements begin and end with T?
Solution

(a) Three S, three T, two I, one A, one C: \(\dfrac{10!}{3!\,3!\,2!}=50{,}400\).

(b) Glue the I's into one block, leaving 9 objects: \(\dfrac{9!}{3!\,3!}=10{,}080\).

(c) Place a T at each end and arrange the remaining S, S, S, T, I, I, A, C: \(\dfrac{8!}{3!\,2!}=3{,}360\).

8 points

\(X\) has density \(f(x)=k(1-x^2)\) for \(-1<x<1\), and \(f(x)=0\) otherwise.

  1. Find \(k\).
  2. Find the CDF of \(X\).
  3. Find \(P\{X>\tfrac12\}\) and \(P\{|X|<\tfrac12\}\).
  4. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
Solution

(a) \(\int_{-1}^1(1-x^2)\,dx=\tfrac43\), so \(k=\tfrac34\).

(b) For \(-1<x<1\): \(F(x)=\tfrac34\left(x-\tfrac{x^3}3\right)+\tfrac12\); \(F=0\) for \(x\le-1\) and \(F=1\) for \(x\ge1\).

(c) \(P\{X>\tfrac12\}=1-F(\tfrac12)=1-\left(\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac{11}{24}+\tfrac12\right)=\tfrac5{32}\). By symmetry \(P\{|X|<\tfrac12\}=1-2\cdot\tfrac5{32}=\tfrac{11}{16}\).

(d) \(E[X]=0\) by symmetry. \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^2]=\tfrac34\int_{-1}^1(x^2-x^4)\,dx=\tfrac34\left(\tfrac23-\tfrac25\right)=\tfrac15\).

7 points

In a soccer league, the number of goals the home team scores in a match is a Poisson random variable with mean 1.5, and the number the away team scores is an independent Poisson random variable with mean 1.0.

  1. Find the probability that a match ends 0–0.
  2. Find the probability that a match ends in a draw with at most one goal per side.
  3. Find the probability that the home team scores in each of its next 3 home matches.
  4. Find the expected total number of goals in 10 matches.
Solution

(a) \(e^{-1.5}e^{-1.0}=e^{-2.5}\approx0.0821\).

(b) 0–0 or 1–1: \(e^{-2.5}+(1.5e^{-1.5})(1.0e^{-1.0})=2.5e^{-2.5}\approx0.2052\).

(c) \((1-e^{-1.5})^3\approx0.4689\).

(d) \(10(1.5+1.0)=25\).

7 points

Four fair six-sided dice are rolled.

  1. Find the probability that all four show different numbers.
  2. Find the probability that exactly two dice match and the other two differ from them and from each other.
  3. Find the probability that the largest number showing is exactly 4.
Solution

(a) \(\dfrac{6\cdot5\cdot4\cdot3}{6^4}=\dfrac{360}{1296}=\dfrac5{18}\).

(b) Choose the paired value (6), which two dice show it \(\binom42=6\), and ordered distinct values for the other two dice \((5\cdot4)\): \(\dfrac{720}{1296}=\dfrac59\).

(c) All at most 4, minus all at most 3: \(\dfrac{4^4-3^4}{6^4}=\dfrac{175}{1296}\approx0.1350\).

7 points

An oil company drills wells one at a time. Each well strikes oil independently with probability 0.2 and costs $2 million to drill.

  1. Find the probability that the first strike comes on the fourth well.
  2. Find the probability that more than 5 wells are needed for the first strike.
  3. Find the probability that the second strike comes on the sixth well.
  4. The company will drill until it has 2 strikes. Find the mean and standard deviation of the total drilling cost.
Solution

(a) \((0.8)^3(0.2)=0.1024\).

(b) The first 5 all miss: \((0.8)^5\approx0.3277\).

(c) \(\binom51(0.2)^2(0.8)^4\approx0.0819\).

(d) The number of wells is negative binomial with \(r=2\), \(p=0.2\): mean \(\tfrac rp=10\), variance \(\tfrac{r(1-p)}{p^2}=40\). Cost: mean $20 million, SD \(2\sqrt{40}\approx\$12.65\) million.

7 points

A machine fills bottles labeled 500 ml. The amount it dispenses is normally distributed with mean 505 ml and standard deviation 4 ml.

  1. Find the probability that a bottle is underfilled, meaning it holds less than 500 ml.
  2. Find the probability that a six-pack contains at least one underfilled bottle, assuming fills are independent.
  3. To what mean should the machine be set so that only 1 percent of bottles are underfilled?
Solution

(a) \(\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{500-505}4\right)=\Phi(-1.25)=1-0.8944=0.1056\).

(b) \(1-(0.8944)^6\approx0.4881\).

(c) Need \(\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{500-\mu}4\right)=0.01\), so \(\tfrac{500-\mu}4=-2.33\) and \(\mu=509.32\) ml.

Thirty percent of incoming email is spam. The word “free” appears in 60 percent of spam and 5 percent of legitimate email. The word “meeting” appears in 10 percent of spam and 40 percent of legitimate email. Given the type of email, the two words appear independently.

  1. An email contains “free”. Find the probability it is spam.
  2. An email contains both words. Find the probability it is spam.
  3. An email contains “free” but not “meeting”. Find the probability it is spam.
Solution

(a) \(\dfrac{0.3(0.6)}{0.3(0.6)+0.7(0.05)}=\dfrac{0.18}{0.215}\approx0.8372\).

(b) \(\dfrac{0.3(0.6)(0.1)}{0.3(0.6)(0.1)+0.7(0.05)(0.4)}=\dfrac{0.018}{0.032}=0.5625\).

(c) \(\dfrac{0.3(0.6)(0.9)}{0.3(0.6)(0.9)+0.7(0.05)(0.6)}=\dfrac{0.162}{0.183}\approx0.8852\).

6 points

A taxi drives from one corner of a street grid to a corner 5 blocks east and 4 blocks north, always moving east or north.

  1. How many different routes are there?
  2. How many routes pass through the corner 2 blocks east and 2 blocks north of the start?
  3. If the driver picks a route at random, all routes equally likely, what is the probability the route avoids that corner?
Solution

(a) Choose which 4 of the 9 blocks go north: \(\binom94=126\).

(b) \(\binom42\binom52=6\cdot10=60\).

(c) \(\dfrac{126-60}{126}=\dfrac{11}{21}\approx0.5238\).

7 points

A box holds 8 light bulbs, 3 of which are defective. Bulbs are tested one at a time, without replacement, until a good bulb is found. Let \(X\) be the number of bulbs tested.

  1. Find the PMF of \(X\) and check that it sums to 1.
  2. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  3. Each test costs $3, and there is a $10 setup charge. Find the mean and variance of the total cost.
Solution

(a) \(p(1)=\tfrac58=\tfrac{35}{56}\); \(p(2)=\tfrac38\cdot\tfrac57=\tfrac{15}{56}\); \(p(3)=\tfrac38\cdot\tfrac27\cdot\tfrac56=\tfrac5{56}\); \(p(4)=\tfrac38\cdot\tfrac27\cdot\tfrac16=\tfrac1{56}\). Sum \(=\tfrac{56}{56}\).

(b) \(E[X]=\tfrac{35+30+15+4}{56}=\tfrac32\). \(E[X^2]=\tfrac{35+60+45+16}{56}=\tfrac{39}{14}\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{39}{14}-\tfrac94=\tfrac{15}{28}\approx0.5357\).

(c) Cost \(=10+3X\): mean \(\$14.50\); variance \(9\cdot\tfrac{15}{28}\approx4.82\).

9 points

Two short problems.

  1. A point \(X\) is chosen uniformly on the interval \((0,10)\), and a square with side \(X\) is drawn. Find the expected area of the square and the probability that the area exceeds 25.
  2. Find the density of the area \(A=X^2\).
  3. The lifetime of a component has density \(\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\) for \(x\ge0\), and half of all components last more than 100 hours. Find \(\lambda\) and the mean lifetime.
  4. For the component in (c), find the probability it lasts more than 300 hours, and the probability it lasts more than 250 hours given that it has lasted 150.
Solution

(a) \(E[X^2]=\int_0^{10}\tfrac{x^2}{10}dx=\tfrac{100}3\approx33.33\). \(P\{X^2>25\}=P\{X>5\}=\tfrac12\).

(b) For \(0<a<100\): \(F_A(a)=P\{X\le\sqrt a\}=\tfrac{\sqrt a}{10}\), so \(f_A(a)=\tfrac1{20\sqrt a}\); 0 otherwise.

(c) \(e^{-100\lambda}=\tfrac12\Rightarrow\lambda=\tfrac{\ln2}{100}\approx0.00693\). Mean \(=\tfrac1\lambda=\tfrac{100}{\ln2}\approx144.27\) hours.

(d) \(P\{X>300\}=\left(\tfrac12\right)^3=\tfrac18\). By memorylessness, \(P\{X>250\mid X>150\}=P\{X>100\}=\tfrac12\).

7 points

Eight married couples, 16 people in all, are seated at random in a row of 16 chairs. Let \(X\) be the number of couples who end up sitting next to each other.

  1. Find the probability that a particular couple sits together.
  2. Find \(E[X]\).
  3. Give a reasonable approximation to the probability that no couple sits together, and say why it is reasonable.
Solution

(a) Treat the couple as a block: \(\dfrac{2\cdot15!}{16!}=\dfrac18\).

(b) \(X=\sum_{i=1}^8I_i\), so \(E[X]=8\cdot\tfrac18=1\). No independence is needed.

(c) Each event has small probability and the events are only weakly dependent, so by the Poisson paradigm \(X\approx\mathrm{Po}(1)\) and \(P\{X=0\}\approx e^{-1}\approx0.3679\).

8 points

A commuter plane has 10 seats. The airline sells 12 tickets at $200 each, and each ticket holder shows up independently with probability 0.85. Every passenger who shows up but cannot be seated is bumped and paid $500 in compensation. The airline keeps all ticket revenue, including from no-shows.

  1. Find the probability that at least one passenger is bumped.
  2. Find the expected number of bumped passengers.
  3. Find the airline's expected net revenue. Does it beat selling exactly 10 tickets?
Solution

(a) \(S\sim\mathrm{Bin}(12,0.85)\). \(P\{S=11\}=12(0.85)^{11}(0.15)\approx0.3012\); \(P\{S=12\}=(0.85)^{12}\approx0.1422\). \(P\{S\ge11\}\approx0.4435\).

(b) \(1(0.3012)+2(0.1422)\approx0.5857\).

(c) \(12(200)-500(0.5857)\approx\$2107\), compared with \(\$2000\) for 10 tickets. Overbooking pays on average.

6 points

A fair die is rolled 180 times. Let \(S\) be the number of sixes.

  1. Approximate \(P\{S\le25\}\).
  2. Approximate \(P\{S=30\}\).
  3. State the condition that justifies your approximation and check it.
Solution

(a) \(np=30\), \(np(1-p)=25\), \(\sigma=5\). With continuity correction, \(P\{S\le25\}=P\{S<25.5\}\approx\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{25.5-30}5\right)=\Phi(-0.9)=1-0.8159=0.1841\).

(b) \(P\{29.5<S<30.5\}\approx\Phi(0.1)-\Phi(-0.1)=2(0.5398)-1=0.0796\).

(c) The normal approximation to the binomial is good when \(np(1-p)\ge10\); here it is 25.
