1
17 points

A salesperson makes phone calls one after another. Each call, independently, results in a sale with probability 0.2.

  1. Find the probability that the first sale comes on the 4th call, and the expected number of calls up to and including the first sale.
  2. Find the probability that the third sale comes on the 10th call, and the expected number of calls needed to make three sales.
  3. The first 5 calls of the morning produce no sale. Find the probability that the first sale takes more than 8 calls in total.
  4. On a given day the salesperson makes exactly 30 calls, earning $50 commission per sale and paying $3 per call. Find the expected value and standard deviation of the day's profit, and the probability that the profit is positive.
Solution

(a) The call of the first sale is geometric with \(p=0.2\): \(P=0.8^3(0.2)=0.1024\); the mean is \(1/p=5\) calls.

(b) The call of the third sale is negative binomial with \(r=3\): \(P=\binom92(0.2)^3(0.8)^7=36(0.008)(0.2097)=0.0604\). The mean is \(r/p=15\) calls.

(c) By the memoryless property of the geometric distribution, \(P(T>8\mid T>5)=P(T>3)=0.8^3=0.512\), where \(T\) is the call of the first sale.

(d) With \(X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(30,0.2)\) sales, the profit is \(50X-90\). \(E=50(6)-90=\$210\); \(\mathrm{SD}=50\sqrt{30(0.2)(0.8)}=50(2.1909)=\$109.54\). The profit is positive when \(50X>90\), i.e. \(X\ge2\): \(1-0.8^{30}-30(0.2)(0.8)^{29}=1-0.0012-0.0093=0.9895\).

2
17 points

A drawer holds 6 pairs of socks, each pair a different colour. Four socks are pulled out at random.

  1. Find the probability that no two of the four socks form a pair.
  2. Find the probability of exactly one complete pair, and of two complete pairs.
  3. Let \(A_i\) be the event that both socks of pair \(i\) are drawn. Find \(P(A_i)\) and \(P(A_iA_j)\), and use inclusion–exclusion to find the probability of at least one complete pair. Check your answer against (a).
  4. Find the expected number of complete pairs drawn, using indicators. Check it against (b).
Solution

There are \(\binom{12}4=495\) equally likely sets of four socks.

(a) Choose 4 of the 6 colours and one sock of each: \(\binom64 2^4=240\). Probability \(\tfrac{240}{495}=\tfrac{16}{33}\).

(b) Exactly one pair: choose the pair (6), then 2 of the other 5 colours with one sock of each, \(\binom52 2^2=40\), for 240 sets, so \(\tfrac{16}{33}\). Two pairs: \(\binom62=15\) sets, so \(\tfrac1{33}\). The three cases sum to \(240+240+15=495\).

(c) \(P(A_i)=\binom{10}2/495=\tfrac{45}{495}=\tfrac1{11}\); \(P(A_iA_j)=\tfrac1{495}\); triple intersections are impossible with only four socks. \(P(\cup A_i)=6\cdot\tfrac{45}{495}-15\cdot\tfrac1{495}=\tfrac{255}{495}=\tfrac{17}{33}=1-\tfrac{16}{33}\). ✓

(d) The number of pairs is \(\sum_{i=1}^6 1_{A_i}\), with expectation \(6\cdot\tfrac1{11}=\tfrac6{11}\). From (b): \(1\cdot\tfrac{16}{33}+2\cdot\tfrac1{33}=\tfrac{18}{33}=\tfrac6{11}\). ✓

3
16 points

A seed company sells packets of 150 seeds. Each seed germinates with probability 0.8, independently of the others. Let \(X\) be the number of seeds in a packet that germinate.

  1. Approximate the probability that at least 128 seeds in a packet germinate. Justify the approximation.
  2. The company wants to print "at least \(k\) seeds will germinate" on the packet and be right for at least 95% of packets. Find the largest such \(k\).
  3. Approximate the probability that fewer than 112 seeds germinate. A gardener buys 3 packets. Find the probability that at least one of them has fewer than 112 seeds germinate.
  4. Packets are shipped in boxes of 20. Find the expected number of packets in a box with at least 128 germinating seeds, and the probability that a box has none.
Solution

\(X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(150,0.8)\), with \(E[X]=120\) and \(\mathrm{SD}=\sqrt{24}=4.899\).

(a) \(np(1-p)=24\ge10\), so use the normal approximation with continuity correction: \(P(X\ge127.5)\approx1-\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{7.5}{4.899}\right)=1-\Phi(1.53)=1-0.9370=0.0630\).

(b) We need \(P(X\ge k)=P(X\ge k-0.5)\ge0.95\), i.e. \(\dfrac{k-0.5-120}{4.899}\le-1.645\), so \(k\le120.5-8.059=112.44\). The largest is \(k=112\).

(c) \(P(X\le111)\approx\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{111.5-120}{4.899}\right)=\Phi(-1.74)=1-0.9591=0.0409\). The packets are independent: \(1-0.9591^3=1-0.8823=0.1177\).

(d) The number of such packets is \(\mathrm{Bin}(20,0.0630)\): expected \(20(0.0630)=1.26\); \(P(\text{none})=0.9370^{20}=0.2721\).

4
16 points

An urn starts with 3 red and 2 blue balls. A ball is drawn at random, then put back together with 2 more balls of the same colour. This is repeated. Let \(R_i\) be the event that draw \(i\) is red.

  1. Find \(P(R_2)\).
  2. Find \(P(R_1\mid R_2)\).
  3. Are \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) independent? Find the probability that the first two draws are the same colour.
  4. Find \(P(R_1R_2R_3)\) and \(P(R_3)\). What do you notice about \(P(R_1)\), \(P(R_2)\), \(P(R_3)\)?
Solution

(a) \(P(R_2)=P(R_2\mid R_1)P(R_1)+P(R_2\mid R_1^c)P(R_1^c)=\tfrac57\cdot\tfrac35+\tfrac37\cdot\tfrac25=\tfrac{15+6}{35}=\tfrac35\).

(b) \(P(R_1\mid R_2)=\dfrac{P(R_1R_2)}{P(R_2)}=\dfrac{(3/5)(5/7)}{3/5}=\tfrac57\).

(c) \(P(R_1R_2)=\tfrac37\ne\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac35=\tfrac9{25}\), so they are not independent: a red draw adds red balls. \(P(\text{same})=\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac57+\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac47=\tfrac{15+8}{35}=\tfrac{23}{35}\).

(d) \(P(R_1R_2R_3)=\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac57\cdot\tfrac79=\tfrac13\). For \(P(R_3)\), condition on the first two draws: RR gives \(\tfrac37\cdot\tfrac79=\tfrac13\); RB gives \(\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac27\cdot\tfrac59=\tfrac2{21}\); BR gives \(\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac37\cdot\tfrac59=\tfrac2{21}\); BB gives \(\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac47\cdot\tfrac39=\tfrac8{105}\). The total is \(\tfrac{35+10+10+8}{105}=\tfrac35\). All three marginal probabilities equal \(\tfrac35\), even though the draws are dependent.

5
16 points

For some constant \(c\), a random variable \(X\) has density \(f(x)=cx^2(3-x)\) for \(0<x<3\) and \(f(x)=0\) otherwise.

  1. Find \(c\).
  2. Find \(E[X]\) and the standard deviation of \(X\).
  3. Find \(P(X<1)\) and \(P(X>2)\).
  4. Let \(Y=\min(X,2)\). Is \(Y\) discrete, continuous, or neither? Find \(E[Y]\).
Solution

(a) \(\int_0^3(3x^2-x^3)dx=27-\tfrac{81}4=\tfrac{27}4\), so \(c=\tfrac4{27}\).

(b) \(E[X]=\tfrac4{27}\int_0^3(3x^3-x^4)dx=\tfrac4{27}\left(\tfrac{243}4-\tfrac{243}5\right)=\tfrac95\). \(E[X^2]=\tfrac4{27}\left(\tfrac{729}5-\tfrac{729}6\right)=\tfrac{18}5\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{18}5-\tfrac{81}{25}=\tfrac9{25}\), so \(\mathrm{SD}=\tfrac35\).

(c) \(F(x)=\tfrac4{27}\left(x^3-\tfrac{x^4}4\right)\). \(P(X<1)=\tfrac4{27}\cdot\tfrac34=\tfrac19\). \(F(2)=\tfrac4{27}(8-4)=\tfrac{16}{27}\), so \(P(X>2)=\tfrac{11}{27}\).

(d) Neither: \(Y\) has a point mass \(P(Y=2)=P(X\ge2)=\tfrac{11}{27}\) and a density on \((0,2)\). $$E[Y]=\int_0^2x f(x)\,dx+2P(X>2)=\tfrac4{27}\left(12-\tfrac{32}5\right)+\tfrac{22}{27}=\tfrac{22.4+22}{27}=\tfrac{74}{45}=1.6444.$$

18 points

A random variable \(X\) has distribution function $$F(x)=\begin{cases}0, & x<0\\ \tfrac{x^2}4, & 0\le x<1\\ \tfrac12, & 1\le x<2\\ \tfrac12+\tfrac{x-2}4, & 2\le x<3\\ 1, & x\ge3.\end{cases}$$

  1. Find every value \(a\) with \(P(X=a)>0\), together with these probabilities. Describe the rest of the distribution.
  2. Find \(P(X<1)\) and \(P(1\le X\le2.5)\).
  3. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  4. Find \(P(X>2\mid X\ge1)\). Find every median of \(X\), i.e. every \(m\) with \(P(X\le m)\ge\tfrac12\) and \(P(X\ge m)\ge\tfrac12\).
Solution

(a) Jumps: at 1, \(\tfrac12-\tfrac14=\tfrac14\); at 3, \(1-\tfrac34=\tfrac14\). \(F\) is continuous everywhere else. The remaining mass has density \(\tfrac x2\) on \((0,1)\) (mass \(\tfrac14\)) and \(\tfrac14\) on \((2,3)\) (mass \(\tfrac14\)), and none on \((1,2)\).

(b) \(P(X<1)=F(1^-)=\tfrac14\). \(P(1\le X\le2.5)=F(2.5)-F(1^-)=\tfrac58-\tfrac14=\tfrac38\).

(c) \(E[X]=1\cdot\tfrac14+3\cdot\tfrac14+\int_0^1\tfrac{x^2}2dx+\int_2^3\tfrac x4dx=1+\tfrac16+\tfrac58=\tfrac{43}{24}=1.7917\). \(E[X^2]=\tfrac14+\tfrac94+\int_0^1\tfrac{x^3}2dx+\int_2^3\tfrac{x^2}4dx=\tfrac52+\tfrac18+\tfrac{19}{12}=\tfrac{101}{24}\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{101}{24}-\left(\tfrac{43}{24}\right)^2=\tfrac{2424-1849}{576}=\tfrac{575}{576}=0.9983\).

(d) \(P(X>2\mid X\ge1)=\dfrac{1-F(2)}{1-F(1^-)}=\dfrac{1/2}{3/4}=\tfrac23\). For \(1\le m\le2\), \(P(X\le m)=\tfrac12\) and \(P(X\ge m)\ge P(X\ge2)=\tfrac12\), so every \(m\in[1,2]\) is a median. Outside that interval one of the two conditions fails, so the medians are exactly \([1,2]\).
