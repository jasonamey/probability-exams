1
8 points

A website requires a 6-character password. Each character is one of the 26 lowercase letters or one of the 10 digits.

  1. How many passwords are possible?
  2. How many passwords have no repeated character?
  3. How many passwords contain at least one digit?
  4. How many passwords contain exactly 2 digits and no repeated character?
Solution

(a) Each of the 6 places has 36 choices: \(36^6=2{,}176{,}782{,}336\).

(b) \(36\cdot35\cdot34\cdot33\cdot32\cdot31=1{,}402{,}410{,}240\).

(c) Complement: subtract the all-letter passwords. \(36^6-26^6=2{,}176{,}782{,}336-308{,}915{,}776=1{,}867{,}866{,}560\).

(d) Choose which 2 places hold digits, \(\binom62=15\); fill them with distinct digits in order, \(10\cdot9\); fill the other 4 with distinct letters in order, \(26\cdot25\cdot24\cdot23\). Total \(15\cdot90\cdot358{,}800=484{,}380{,}000\).

2
8 points

Consider the letters of the word COMMITTEE (9 letters, with M, T and E each appearing twice).

  1. How many distinguishable arrangements of the letters are there?
  2. How many of them begin and end with E?
  3. How many of them have the two M's next to each other?
  4. How many of them have the vowels (O, I, E, E) appearing in the left-to-right order E, E, I, O, not necessarily next to each other?
Solution

(a) \(\dfrac{9!}{2!\,2!\,2!}=45{,}360\).

(b) The ends are fixed as E; arrange C, O, M, M, I, T, T in the middle: \(\dfrac{7!}{2!\,2!}=1260\).

(c) Glue MM into one block. Arrange 8 units (C, O, MM, I, T, T, E, E): \(\dfrac{8!}{2!\,2!}=10{,}080\).

(d) Choose the 4 positions for the vowels, \(\binom94=126\); the order of the vowels in them is forced. Arrange C, M, M, T, T in the remaining 5 positions: \(\dfrac{5!}{2!\,2!}=30\). Total \(126\cdot30=3780\). (Check: the vowels can appear in \(4!/2!=12\) orders equally often, and \(45{,}360/12=3780\).)

3
8 points

Eight teams enter a regional league.

  1. In the regular season every team plays every other team exactly once. How many games are played?
  2. For the opening day, the 8 teams are split into 4 games (pairs of opponents). How many different opening-day schedules are possible?
  3. Gold, silver and bronze medals go to three different teams. In how many ways can the medals be awarded?
  4. At the end of the season the top 4 teams (as a set, unordered) qualify for the playoffs. In how many ways can the qualifying set include team A but not team B?
Solution

(a) Each game is an unordered pair of teams: \(\binom82=28\).

(b) Divide 8 teams into 4 unlabelled groups of 2: \(\dfrac{8!}{(2!)^4\,4!}=\dfrac{40{,}320}{16\cdot24}=105\).

(c) Ordered selection of 3 from 8: \(8\cdot7\cdot6=336\).

(d) A is in and B is out, so choose the other 3 qualifiers from the remaining 6 teams: \(\binom63=20\).

9 points

Six cards are dealt from a well-shuffled standard 52-card deck. Count the number of 6-card hands (order does not matter) of each type.

  1. All 6-card hands.
  2. Hands with exactly 3 hearts.
  3. Hands with exactly 2 cards in each of three suits (so the fourth suit is missing).
  4. Hands consisting of three pairs: three different ranks, two cards of each.
Solution

(a) \(\binom{52}6=20{,}358{,}520\).

(b) \(\binom{13}3\binom{39}3=286\cdot9139=2{,}613{,}754\).

(c) Choose the 3 suits present, \(\binom43=4\), then 2 cards from each: \(4\binom{13}2^3=4\cdot78^3=1{,}898{,}208\).

(d) Choose the 3 ranks, \(\binom{13}3=286\), then 2 of the 4 suits for each rank: \(286\cdot\binom42^3=286\cdot216=61{,}776\).

8 points

A sandwich shop offers 3 kinds of bread, 5 meats, 6 vegetables and 4 sauces. A sandwich is made of one bread, either 1 or 2 different meats, any collection of vegetables (possibly none), and at most one sauce.

  1. How many different sandwiches are possible?
  2. How many of them have exactly 3 vegetables?
  3. The shop adds a vegetarian option: a sandwich may now have 0, 1 or 2 different meats (everything else unchanged). How many sandwiches are possible now?
  4. Under the original rules, how many sandwiches have at least one vegetable and exactly one sauce?
Solution

(a) Bread 3; meats \(\binom51+\binom52=15\); vegetables: each is in or out, \(2^6=64\); sauce: none or one of 4, so 5. Total \(3\cdot15\cdot64\cdot5=14{,}400\).

(b) Replace 64 by \(\binom63=20\): \(3\cdot15\cdot20\cdot5=4500\).

(c) Meats \(1+5+10=16\): \(3\cdot16\cdot64\cdot5=15{,}360\).

(d) Vegetables \(2^6-1=63\), sauce 4: \(3\cdot15\cdot63\cdot4=11{,}340\).

9 points

Twelve volunteers, including Ana and Ben, are to be assigned to three sites. The food bank needs 5 people, the shelter needs 4, and the park clean-up needs 3.

  1. How many assignments are possible?
  2. In how many assignments are Ana and Ben at the same site?
  3. On another day the 12 volunteers are simply split into three teams of 4 with no site attached (the teams are not labelled). How many splits are possible?
  4. On a third day they are split into 6 unlabelled pairs. How many ways is that possible?
Solution

(a) Multinomial coefficient: \(\dbinom{12}{5,4,3}=\dfrac{12!}{5!\,4!\,3!}=27{,}720\).

(b) Split by site. Both at the food bank: assign the other 10 as \((3,4,3)\), \(\frac{10!}{3!4!3!}=4200\). Both at the shelter: \((5,2,3)\), \(\frac{10!}{5!2!3!}=2520\). Both at the park: \((5,4,1)\), \(\frac{10!}{5!4!1!}=1260\). Total \(7980\).

(c) Labelled split \(\frac{12!}{4!\,4!\,4!}=34{,}650\), then divide by the \(3!\) orderings of identical-size teams: \(34{,}650/6=5775\).

(d) \(\dfrac{12!}{(2!)^6\,6!}=\dfrac{479{,}001{,}600}{64\cdot720}=10{,}395\).

8 points

Consider the four-digit numbers 1000 through 9999.

  1. How many have four different digits?
  2. How many are even and have four different digits?
  3. How many have digits that strictly increase from left to right (for example 1359)?
  4. How many contain at least one digit 7?
Solution

(a) First digit 1–9 (9 ways), then 9, 8, 7 choices: \(9\cdot9\cdot8\cdot7=4536\).

(b) Fill the last digit first. Last digit 0: \(9\cdot8\cdot7=504\). Last digit 2, 4, 6 or 8: the first digit cannot be 0 or the last digit (8 ways), then 8 and 7: \(4\cdot8\cdot8\cdot7=1792\). Total \(2296\).

(c) 0 cannot appear (it would have to be the first digit). Any 4 distinct digits from 1–9 can be arranged increasingly in exactly one way: \(\binom94=126\).

(d) Complement: numbers with no 7 have first digit from 8 choices and the rest from 9 each: \(8\cdot9^3=5832\). So \(9000-5832=3168\).

9 points

Answer the following without expanding anything by hand.

  1. Find the coefficient of \(x^3y^5\) in \((2x-y)^8\).
  2. Find the coefficient of \(a^2b^3c\) in \((a+b+2c)^6\).
  3. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{10}\binom{10}{k}2^k\).
  4. A club has 10 seniors and 8 juniors. Write the number of 4-person committees as a sum over the number of seniors on the committee, and use it to evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{10}{k}\binom{8}{4-k}\).
Solution

(a) The term is \(\binom83(2x)^3(-y)^5\), so the coefficient is \(56\cdot8\cdot(-1)=-448\).

(b) Multinomial theorem: \(\dbinom{6}{2,3,1}a^2b^3(2c)^1\), coefficient \(\dfrac{6!}{2!\,3!\,1!}\cdot2=60\cdot2=120\).

(c) Binomial theorem with \(x=2,\ y=1\): \((2+1)^{10}=3^{10}=59{,}049\).

(d) A committee with \(k\) seniors is formed in \(\binom{10}k\binom8{4-k}\) ways; summing over \(k=0,\dots,4\) counts every 4-person committee from 18 people once. So the sum equals \(\binom{18}4=3060\).

8 points

A courier must visit six stops, labelled A through F, once each, in some order.

  1. How many routes (orders of visiting the stops) are possible?
  2. In how many routes is A visited before B (not necessarily immediately)?
  3. In how many routes are A, B and C visited in that relative order?
  4. In how many routes are A and B not visited consecutively?
Solution

(a) \(6!=720\).

(b) Swapping A and B pairs each route with A before B with one with B before A, so half: \(360\).

(c) The \(3!=6\) relative orders of A, B, C are equally common: \(720/6=120\).

(d) Routes with A and B adjacent: glue them into a block (2 internal orders) and arrange 5 units: \(2\cdot5!=240\). So \(720-240=480\).

8 points

A DNA strand of length 10 is a sequence of 10 letters, each A, C, G or T.

  1. How many strands are possible?
  2. How many strands contain exactly 3 A's, 3 C's, 2 G's and 2 T's?
  3. How many strands contain exactly 4 A's?
  4. How many strands read the same forwards and backwards?
Solution

(a) \(4^{10}=1{,}048{,}576\).

(b) Permutations with repetition: \(\dfrac{10!}{3!\,3!\,2!\,2!}=25{,}200\).

(c) Choose the positions of the A's, \(\binom{10}4=210\); each other position is C, G or T: \(210\cdot3^6=153{,}090\).

(d) The first 5 letters determine the last 5: \(4^5=1024\).

9 points

Seven different toys are given to three children, Alice, Bob and Cal. Every toy goes to some child.

  1. In how many ways can the toys be distributed?
  2. In how many ways can Alice get 3 toys, Bob 2 and Cal 2?
  3. In how many ways can every child get at least one toy?
  4. In how many ways does Cal get exactly 2 toys?
Solution

(a) Each toy has 3 possible recipients: \(3^7=2187\).

(b) \(\dbinom{7}{3,2,2}=\dfrac{7!}{3!\,2!\,2!}=210\).

(c) List the possible shapes of the split and how many ways to assign the sizes to children. \((5,1,1)\): \(\frac{7!}{5!1!1!}=42\), times 3 = 126. \((4,2,1)\): \(\frac{7!}{4!2!1!}=105\), times 6 = 630. \((3,3,1)\): \(\frac{7!}{3!3!1!}=140\), times 3 = 420. \((3,2,2)\): 210, times 3 = 630. Total \(1806\). (Equivalently \(3^7-3\cdot2^7+3=1806\).)

(d) Choose Cal's 2 toys, \(\binom72=21\); each of the other 5 goes to Alice or Bob: \(21\cdot2^5=672\).

8 points

Nine different books are placed in a row on a shelf: 4 novels, 3 biographies and 2 dictionaries.

  1. How many arrangements are possible?
  2. How many arrangements keep the books of each type together?
  3. How many arrangements have a dictionary at each end?
  4. How many arrangements have no two biographies next to each other?
Solution

(a) \(9!=362{,}880\).

(b) Order the three blocks (\(3!\)) and the books inside each block: \(3!\cdot4!\cdot3!\cdot2!=6\cdot24\cdot6\cdot2=1728\).

(c) The two dictionaries at the ends in \(2\) orders, the other 7 books in between in \(7!\) ways: \(2\cdot5040=10{,}080\).

(d) Arrange the 6 non-biographies (\(6!=720\)), creating 7 gaps (including both ends). Place the 3 distinct biographies in 3 different gaps in order: \(7\cdot6\cdot5=210\). Total \(720\cdot210=151{,}200\).
