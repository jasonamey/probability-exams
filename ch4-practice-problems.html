Chapter 4 Practice Problems: Random Variables

22 worked-solution problems modeled on the Chapter 4 slides and HW 4 and HW 5. Each one is a variation, with new numbers or an added part, so you have to redo the reasoning. Work each on paper first, then open the solution.

Round decimals to 4 places unless a fraction is natural.
1

Five cards are dealt from a standard 52-card deck. Let \(X\) be the number of aces.

  1. Using indicators and the result of Problem 5, find \(E[X]\) and \(E[X^2]\).
  2. Find \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\) and confirm it matches the hypergeometric variance formula.
Solution

(a) \(I_i\) = card \(i\) is an ace, \(p_i=\tfrac4{52}=\tfrac1{13}\). \(E[X]=\tfrac5{13}\approx0.3846\). For \(i\ne j\), \(P(I_i=I_j=1)=\tfrac4{52}\cdot\tfrac3{51}=\tfrac1{221}\), so \(E[X^2]=\tfrac5{13}+20\cdot\tfrac1{221}=\tfrac{105}{221}\approx0.4751\).

(b) \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{105}{221}-\tfrac{25}{169}\approx0.3272\). Formula: \(5\cdot\tfrac1{13}\cdot\tfrac{12}{13}\cdot\tfrac{47}{51}\approx0.3272\). ✓

2

The number of colds a person contracts in a year is Poisson with \(\lambda=4\). A new drug lowers the parameter to \(\lambda=2\) for 60 percent of the population and has no effect on the other 40 percent. A person takes the drug for a year.

  1. If they have exactly 1 cold that year, how likely is it that the drug is beneficial for them?
  2. If they have 0 colds?
  3. What is the unconditional probability that they have 0 colds?
Solution

Bayes with \(B\) = drug is beneficial.

(a) \(\displaystyle P(B\mid1)=\frac{0.6\cdot2e^{-2}}{0.6\cdot2e^{-2}+0.4\cdot4e^{-4}}=\frac{0.16240}{0.16240+0.02930}\approx0.8471\).

(b) \(\displaystyle P(B\mid0)=\frac{0.6e^{-2}}{0.6e^{-2}+0.4e^{-4}}\approx0.9172\).

(c) \(0.6e^{-2}+0.4e^{-4}\approx0.0812+0.0073=0.0885\).

3

Binomial properties.

  1. Let \(X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(10,0.4)\). Find \(E[X]\), \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\), the most likely value of \(X\), and the probability of that value.
  2. A binomial random variable \(Y\) has \(E[Y]=6\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(Y)=2.4\). Find \(n\) and \(p\), then \(P\{Y\ge9\}\).
Solution

(a) \(E[X]=4\), \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=2.4\). The mode is \(\lfloor(n+1)p\rfloor=\lfloor4.4\rfloor=4\); \(P\{X=4\}=\binom{10}4(0.4)^4(0.6)^6\approx0.2508\).

(b) \(1-p=\frac{2.4}{6}=0.4\Rightarrow p=0.6\), \(n=10\). \(P\{Y\ge9\}=10(0.6)^9(0.4)+(0.6)^{10}\approx0.0464\).

4

A buyer purchases components in lots of size 12. The policy is to inspect 4 components at random from a lot and accept the lot only if all 4 are non-defective. Forty percent of lots have 3 defective components and 60 percent have 1.

  1. What proportion of lots does the buyer reject?
  2. Given that a lot was rejected, what is the probability it had 3 defectives?
Solution

(a) \(P(\text{accept}\mid3\text{ def})=\binom94\big/\binom{12}4=\tfrac{126}{495}=\tfrac{14}{55}\). \(P(\text{accept}\mid1\text{ def})=\binom{11}4\big/\binom{12}4=\tfrac{330}{495}=\tfrac23\).

\(P(\text{reject})=0.4\cdot\tfrac{41}{55}+0.6\cdot\tfrac13=\tfrac{137}{275}\approx0.4982\).

(b) \(\dfrac{0.4\cdot\frac{41}{55}}{\frac{137}{275}}=\dfrac{82}{137}\approx0.5985\).

5

Let \(X\) be the number of successes in \(n\) trials, where trial \(i\) is a success with probability \(p_i\). The trials need not be independent.

  1. Find \(E[X]\).
  2. Derive an expression for \(E[X^2]\).
  3. Use (a) and (b) to obtain the mean and variance of \(\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\).
  4. A professor returns 20 graded exams to 20 students completely at random. Find the expected number of students who get their own exam.
Solution

Write \(X=\sum_{i=1}^nI_i\), where \(I_i\) indicates success on trial \(i\).

(a) \(E[X]=\sum_iE[I_i]=\sum_ip_i\).

(b) \(X^2=\sum_iI_i^2+\sum_{i\ne j}I_iI_j\), and \(I_i^2=I_i\), so \(E[X^2]=\sum_ip_i+\sum_{i\ne j}P(\text{trials }i\text{ and }j\text{ both succeed})\).

(c) Independent trials with \(p_i=p\): \(E[X]=np\); \(E[X^2]=np+n(n-1)p^2\); \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=np+n(n-1)p^2-n^2p^2=np(1-p)\).

(d) \(p_i=\tfrac1{20}\) for each student, so \(E[X]=20\cdot\tfrac1{20}=1\).

6

The numbers of daily accidents on three highways are Poisson random variables with parameters 0.4, 0.6, and 1.0.

  1. Find the expected total number of accidents on these highways today.
  2. Find the expected total over a 7-day week.
  3. Let \(X\sim\mathrm{Po}(1.0)\) be the count on the third highway. The daily cleanup cost is \(C=200+150X+50X^2\) dollars. Find \(E[C]\).
  4. Did part (a) need independence? Explain.
Solution

(a) \(E[X_1+X_2+X_3]=0.4+0.6+1.0=2.0\).

(b) \(7\times2=14\).

(c) \(E[X^2]=\mathrm{Var}(X)+(E[X])^2=1+1=2\). \(E[C]=200+150(1)+50(2)=\$450\).

(d) No. \(E\left[\sum X_i\right]=\sum E[X_i]\) holds for any random variables, dependent or not.

7

A batch of 50 items contains 5 defectives. A sample of 8 items is drawn at random without replacement; \(X\) is the number of defectives in the sample.

  1. Name the distribution of \(X\) and its parameters.
  2. Find \(P\{X=0\}\) and \(P\{X\ge2\}\).
  3. Find \(E[X]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  4. Approximate \(P\{X=0\}\) with a binomial. Why is the approximation rough here?
Solution

(a) \(X\sim\mathrm{hypergeo}(N=50,\,n=8,\,m=5)\).

(b) \(P\{X=0\}=\binom{45}8\big/\binom{50}8\approx0.4015\). \(P\{X=1\}=5\binom{45}7\big/\binom{50}8\approx0.4226\). \(P\{X\ge2\}\approx1-0.4015-0.4226=0.1759\).

(c) \(E[X]=8\cdot\tfrac5{50}=0.8\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=8(0.1)(0.9)\cdot\tfrac{42}{49}\approx0.6171\).

(d) \(\mathrm{Bin}(8,0.1)\): \(0.9^8\approx0.4305\). The sample is 16 percent of the batch, so \(N\) and \(m\) are not large relative to \(n\).

8

A box contains 4 red and 6 blue marbles. Two marbles are withdrawn at random. If they are the same color you win $1.20; if they are different colors you lose $1.00.

  1. Find the expected value of the amount you win.
  2. Find the variance of the amount you win.
Solution

\(P(\text{same})=\frac{\binom42+\binom62}{\binom{10}2}=\frac{6+15}{45}=\frac7{15}\), \(P(\text{different})=\frac8{15}\).

(a) \(E[X]=1.20\cdot\tfrac7{15}-1.00\cdot\tfrac8{15}=\tfrac{0.4}{15}=\tfrac2{75}\approx0.0267\).

(b) \(E[X^2]=1.44\cdot\tfrac7{15}+1\cdot\tfrac8{15}=\tfrac{18.08}{15}\approx1.2053\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=1.2053-(0.0267)^2\approx1.2046\).

9

The Banach match problem. A mathematician carries two matchboxes, one in each pocket, each initially holding \(N\) matches. Each time a match is needed, either pocket is equally likely to be chosen. Consider the moment the mathematician first discovers that one box is empty.

  1. Find the probability that exactly \(k\) matches remain in the other box, \(k=0,1,\dots,N\).
  2. Evaluate it for \(N=3\) and \(k=0,1,2,3\), and check the values sum to 1.
Solution

(a) Let \(E\) = the right box is found empty with \(k\) left in the left box. That happens when the \((N+1)\)st choice of the right pocket occurs on trial \(N+1+N-k=2N-k+1\). By the negative binomial with \(r=N+1\), \(p=\tfrac12\):

$$P(E)=\binom{2N-k}{N}\left(\tfrac12\right)^{2N-k+1}.$$

By symmetry the answer is \(2P(E)=\binom{2N-k}{N}\left(\tfrac12\right)^{2N-k}\).

(b) \(k=0\): \(\tfrac{20}{64}\); \(k=1\): \(\tfrac{10}{32}=\tfrac{20}{64}\); \(k=2\): \(\tfrac4{16}=\tfrac{16}{64}\); \(k=3\): \(\tfrac18=\tfrac8{64}\). Sum \(=\tfrac{64}{64}=1\). ✓

10

A 400-page book is typeset so that each page independently contains at least one typo with probability 0.005.

  1. Use the Poisson approximation to find the probability that at least 3 pages contain a typo.
  2. Why is the approximation reasonable here? Write the exact expression it approximates.
Solution

(a) \(\lambda=400(0.005)=2\). \(P\{X\ge3\}\approx1-e^{-2}\left(1+2+\tfrac{4}{2}\right)=1-5e^{-2}\approx0.3233\).

(b) \(n\) is large, \(p\) is small, \(np=2\) is moderate. Exact: \(1-\sum_{i=0}^2\binom{400}i(0.005)^i(0.995)^{400-i}\approx0.3233\).

11

Let \(X\sim\mathrm{geo}(p)\).

  1. Show that \(P\{X\ge k\}=(1-p)^{k-1}\) for \(k=1,2,\dots\)
  2. Use (a) to prove the memoryless property: \(P\{X\ge n+k\mid X>n\}=P\{X\ge k\}\) for \(n\ge0\), \(k\ge1\).
  3. Write the CDF of \(X\).
Solution

(a) \(X\ge k\) iff the first \(k-1\) trials all fail, probability \((1-p)^{k-1}\). (Or sum the geometric series \(\sum_{n\ge k}(1-p)^{n-1}p\).)

(b) \(\{X>n\}=\{X\ge n+1\}\), which contains \(\{X\ge n+k\}\). So the conditional probability is \(\dfrac{(1-p)^{n+k-1}}{(1-p)^{n}}=(1-p)^{k-1}=P\{X\ge k\}\).

(c) \(F(x)=1-(1-p)^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\) for \(x\ge1\), and \(F(x)=0\) for \(x<1\).

12

Screws produced by a company are defective with probability 0.02, independently of one another. The screws are sold in packages of 20 with a money-back guarantee that at most 1 of the 20 is defective.

  1. What proportion of packages must the company replace? Give the exact answer.
  2. Redo (a) with the Poisson approximation.
  3. A customer buys 3 packages. What is the probability exactly one is returned? Use the answer from (a).
Solution

(a) \(X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(20,0.02)\). \(P\{X>1\}=1-0.98^{20}-20(0.02)(0.98)^{19}\approx1-0.6676-0.2725=0.0599\).

(b) \(\lambda=np=0.4\). \(P\{X>1\}\approx1-e^{-0.4}(1+0.4)\approx0.0616\).

(c) Number returned is \(\mathrm{Bin}(3,0.0599)\): \(3(0.0599)(0.9401)^2\approx0.1588\).

13

A player bets on one of the numbers 1 through 6. Three fair dice are rolled independently. If the player's number appears \(i\) times, \(i=1,2,3\), the player wins \(i\) units; if it does not appear, the player loses 1 unit.

  1. Is the game fair to the player?
  2. Find the variance of the player's winnings.
  3. Keeping the other payoffs fixed, what payoff for three matches would make the game fair?
Solution

The number of matches is \(\mathrm{Bin}(3,\tfrac16)\): probabilities \(\tfrac{125}{216},\tfrac{75}{216},\tfrac{15}{216},\tfrac1{216}\) for \(0,1,2,3\) matches.

(a) \(E[X]=\frac{-125+75+30+3}{216}=-\frac{17}{216}\approx-0.0787\). Not fair; the player loses on average.

(b) \(E[X^2]=\frac{125+75+60+9}{216}=\frac{269}{216}\); \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\frac{269}{216}-\left(\frac{17}{216}\right)^2\approx1.2392\).

(c) Need \(-125+75+30+w=0\Rightarrow w=20\) units.

14

Four men and 6 women are ranked according to their exam scores. No two scores are alike and all \(10!\) rankings are equally likely. Let \(X\) be the highest ranking achieved by a man (so \(X=1\) if the top-ranked person is male).

  1. Find \(P\{X=i\}\) for \(i=1,2,\dots,10\).
  2. Verify the probabilities sum to 1.
  3. Find \(E[X]\).
Solution

Only the set of positions held by the 4 men matters: \(\binom{10}{4}=210\) equally likely sets. \(X=i\) means a man is at rank \(i\) and the other 3 men are among the \(10-i\) lower ranks:

$$P\{X=i\}=\frac{\binom{10-i}{3}}{\binom{10}{4}},\quad i=1,\dots,7;\qquad P\{X=i\}=0,\ i=8,9,10.$$

Values: \(\tfrac{84}{210}=\tfrac25,\ \tfrac{56}{210}=\tfrac4{15},\ \tfrac{35}{210}=\tfrac16,\ \tfrac{20}{210}=\tfrac2{21},\ \tfrac{10}{210}=\tfrac1{21},\ \tfrac4{210}=\tfrac2{105},\ \tfrac1{210}\).

(b) \(84+56+35+20+10+4+1=210\). ✓

(c) \(E[X]=\frac{1(84)+2(56)+3(35)+4(20)+5(10)+6(4)+7(1)}{210}=\frac{462}{210}=2.2\).

15

The CDF of \(X\) is

$$F(x)=\begin{cases}0 & x<0\\[2pt] x/3 & 0\le x<1\\[2pt] 1/2 & 1\le x<2\\[2pt] 5/6 & 2\le x<4\\[2pt] 1 & x\ge 4\end{cases}$$
  1. Sketch \(F\) and check the four properties of a CDF.
  2. Find \(P\{X=1\}\), \(P\{X=2\}\), \(P\{X=4\}\), and \(P\{X=3\}\).
  3. Find \(P\{X<2\}\), \(P\{X\ge 1\}\), \(P\{\tfrac12<X\le 2\}\), and \(P\{\tfrac12<X<2\}\).
  4. Is \(X\) a discrete random variable? Explain.
Solution

(a) Nondecreasing, \(F\to1\) as \(x\to\infty\), \(F\to0\) as \(x\to-\infty\), and right continuous at each jump (the value at the jump point is the upper value).

(b) A point mass is the jump size, \(P\{X=a\}=F(a)-F(a^-)\): \(P\{X=1\}=\tfrac12-\tfrac13=\tfrac16\); \(P\{X=2\}=\tfrac56-\tfrac12=\tfrac13\); \(P\{X=4\}=1-\tfrac56=\tfrac16\); \(P\{X=3\}=0\) (no jump).

(c) \(P\{X<2\}=F(2^-)=\tfrac12\). \(P\{X\ge1\}=1-F(1^-)=1-\tfrac13=\tfrac23\). \(P\{\tfrac12<X\le2\}=F(2)-F(\tfrac12)=\tfrac56-\tfrac16=\tfrac23\). \(P\{\tfrac12<X<2\}=F(2^-)-F(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12-\tfrac16=\tfrac13\).

(d) No. The point masses total \(\tfrac16+\tfrac13+\tfrac16=\tfrac23\); the remaining \(\tfrac13\) is spread continuously over \((0,1)\), so \(F\) is not a pure step function.

16

A coin with \(P(\text{heads})=p\in(0,1)\) is flipped until either a head occurs or a total of \(n\) flips has been made. Let \(X\) be the number of flips.

  1. Find \(P\{X=i\}\), \(i=1,\dots,n\).
  2. Verify that \(\sum_{i=1}^n P\{X=i\}=1\).
  3. Find \(E[X]\) when \(n=4\) and \(p=\tfrac13\).
Solution

(a) With \(q=1-p\): \(P\{X=i\}=q^{i-1}p\) for \(i=1,\dots,n-1\), and \(P\{X=n\}=q^{n-1}\) (the first \(n-1\) flips are tails; the last flip can be either).

(b) \(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q^{i-1}p=p\cdot\frac{1-q^{n-1}}{1-q}=1-q^{n-1}\), and adding \(q^{n-1}\) gives 1.

(c) PMF: \(\tfrac13,\ \tfrac29,\ \tfrac4{27},\ \tfrac8{27}\). \(E[X]=\tfrac{9+12+12+32}{27}=\tfrac{65}{27}\approx2.4074\). (In general \(E[X]=\frac{1-q^n}{p}\).)

17

\(X\) takes the values \(-2, 0, 1, 3\) with probabilities \(0.1, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2\).

  1. Find \(E[X]\), \(E[X^2]\), \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\), and \(\mathrm{SD}(X)\).
  2. Find \(E[3X-2]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(3X-2)\).
  3. Find \(E[|X|]\) and \(E[2^X]\).
  4. Find the PMF of \(Y=X^2\) and recompute \(E[X^2]\) from it.
Solution

(a) \(E[X]=-0.2+0+0.3+0.6=0.7\). \(E[X^2]=0.4+0+0.3+1.8=2.5\). \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=2.5-0.49=2.01\). \(\mathrm{SD}(X)=\sqrt{2.01}\approx1.4177\).

(b) \(E[3X-2]=3(0.7)-2=0.1\). \(\mathrm{Var}(3X-2)=9(2.01)=18.09\).

(c) \(E[|X|]=0.2+0+0.3+0.6=1.1\). \(E[2^X]=\tfrac14(0.1)+1(0.4)+2(0.3)+8(0.2)=2.625\).

(d) \(Y\) takes \(0,1,4,9\) with probabilities \(0.4,0.3,0.1,0.2\); \(E[Y]=0.3+0.4+1.8=2.5\). ✓

18

A roulette wheel has 38 numbers: 1 through 36, 0, and double 0. Jones always bets that the outcome will be one of the six numbers 1 through 6.

  1. Find the probability that Jones loses the first 4 bets.
  2. Find the probability that the first win occurs on the third bet.
  3. Find the expected number of bets until the first win, and its variance.
  4. Given that Jones has lost the first 10 bets, find the probability that the first win takes at least 3 more bets.
  5. Find the probability that Jones's second win occurs on the sixth bet.
Solution

\(p=\tfrac6{38}=\tfrac3{19}\), \(q=\tfrac{16}{19}\); \(X\sim\mathrm{geo}(p)\).

(a) \(q^4\approx0.5029\).

(b) \(q^2p=\left(\tfrac{16}{19}\right)^2\tfrac3{19}\approx0.1120\).

(c) \(E[X]=\tfrac1p=\tfrac{19}3\approx6.33\); \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\tfrac{q}{p^2}=\tfrac{304}{9}\approx33.78\).

(d) Memoryless: \(P\{X\ge13\mid X>10\}=P\{X\ge3\}=q^2\approx0.7091\).

(e) Negative binomial with \(r=2\): \(\binom51p^2q^4\approx0.0627\).

19

Customers enter a bank according to the three Poisson assumptions at a rate of 3 per hour.

  1. Find the probability that at least 2 customers enter in the next 30 minutes.
  2. Let \(T\) be the time in hours until the next customer enters. Find the CDF of \(T\).
  3. Find the probability that no customer enters in the next 20 minutes.
Solution

\(N(t)\sim\mathrm{Po}(3t)\).

(a) \(N(0.5)\sim\mathrm{Po}(1.5)\): \(1-e^{-1.5}(1+1.5)\approx0.4422\).

(b) \(\{T>t\}=\{N(t)=0\}\), so \(P\{T>t\}=e^{-3t}\) and \(F(t)=1-e^{-3t}\) for \(t\ge0\), with \(F(t)=0\) for \(t<0\).

(c) \(P\{T>\tfrac13\}=e^{-1}\approx0.3679\).

20

The PMF of \(X\) is \(p(i)=c\left(\tfrac13\right)^i\), \(i=0,1,2,\dots\)

  1. Find \(c\).
  2. Find \(P\{X=0\}\) and \(P\{X>2\}\).
  3. Find the probability that \(X\) is even.
Solution

(a) \(\sum_{i\ge0}c(1/3)^i=c\cdot\frac{1}{1-1/3}=\tfrac32c=1\Rightarrow c=\tfrac23\).

(b) \(P\{X=0\}=\tfrac23\). \(P\{X>2\}=\sum_{i\ge3}\tfrac23(\tfrac13)^i=(\tfrac13)^3=\tfrac1{27}\).

(c) \(\sum_{k\ge0}\tfrac23(\tfrac19)^k=\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac98=\tfrac34\).

21

An interviewer needs to interview 4 people. Each person on a list agrees to be interviewed, independently, with probability \(\tfrac34\). The interviewer works down the list and stops once 4 interviews are done.

  1. What is the probability the list is long enough if it has 4 people?
  2. If it has 6 people?
  3. With the 6-person list, what is the probability the interviewer speaks to exactly 5 people?
  4. Exactly 6 people and gets the 4 interviews?
Solution

(a) \((\tfrac34)^4=\tfrac{81}{256}\approx0.3164\).

(b) \(P\{\mathrm{Bin}(6,\tfrac34)\ge4\}=\frac{15\cdot3^4+6\cdot3^5+3^6}{4^6}=\frac{3402}{4096}=\frac{1701}{2048}\approx0.8306\).

(c) The 4th success comes on trial 5, a negative binomial: \(\binom43(\tfrac34)^4(\tfrac14)=\tfrac{81}{256}\).

(d) \(\binom53(\tfrac34)^4(\tfrac14)^2=\tfrac{405}{2048}\approx0.1978\). Check: \(\tfrac{648+648+405}{2048}=\tfrac{1701}{2048}\), matching (b).

22

Short derivations.

  1. Starting from \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[(X-\mu)^2]\), show \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2\).
  2. Show \(\mathrm{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\,\mathrm{Var}(X)\).
  3. Let \(I_A\) be the indicator of event \(A\). Find \(E[I_A]\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(I_A)\).
  4. Explain why \(E[X^2]\ge(E[X])^2\) for every random variable. When does equality hold?
Solution

(a) \(E[(X-\mu)^2]=E[X^2-2\mu X+\mu^2]=E[X^2]-2\mu^2+\mu^2=E[X^2]-\mu^2\).

(b) \(E[aX+b]=a\mu+b\), so \(\mathrm{Var}(aX+b)=E[(aX+b-a\mu-b)^2]=a^2E[(X-\mu)^2]=a^2\mathrm{Var}(X)\).

(c) \(E[I_A]=P(A)\). Since \(I_A^2=I_A\), \(\mathrm{Var}(I_A)=P(A)-P(A)^2=P(A)(1-P(A))\).

(d) \(E[X^2]-(E[X])^2=\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[(X-\mu)^2]\ge0\). Equality holds iff \(X\) is constant almost surely.
